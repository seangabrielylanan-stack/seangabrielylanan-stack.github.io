03 / PROJECTS

What I've built

COURSE PROJECT · SDG 11

V2I-Mesh: Solar-Powered Off-Grid Public Transit Monitoring & Commuter Information System

A decentralized transit information network for urban barangays. Vehicles talk directly to roadside stops over low-power radio, so commuters see live arrival times and open seats without internet, mobile data or a smartphone.

1 Count seats→2 Broadcast→3 Compute ETA→4 Display

Key features

  • 👥 Door sensors count seats, no cameras
  • 📡 Direct radio broadcast to stops
  • ⏱️ Kinematic arrival-time estimates
  • 🖥️ Sunlight-readable e-paper display
  • ☀️ Solar + battery energy autonomy
  • 🔁 Mesh relay around blocked signals

Tools & technologies

  • Door sensors
  • Low-power radio
  • E-paper
  • Solar power
  • Mesh networking

Team: Raymund V. B. Sabado, Berthe-Annie Jean Louis, Sean Gabriel F. Ylanan

04 / PERSONAL JOURNEY

My story at Mapúa

  • Why I started

    Ever since I was younger, I've always been curious about how things worked behind the scenes. I liked taking things apart and figuring out the logic behind them. But my real interest in tech started clicking during the pandemic. With all that extra time at home, I ended up watching tons of videos about computers and engineering, and I realized how fast the tech world was moving. That made me decide I wanted to actually be part of building it.

  • The challenges

    Of course, adjusting to college hasn't been a breeze. The biggest challenge was getting used to how fast everything moves. In high school, you get a lot of guidance, but in college, you really have to learn how to pick things up quickly on your own. Group projects have also been a bit different. Back in senior high, managing group tasks felt pretty straightforward, but at the university level, getting everyone on the same page and coordinating schedules takes a lot more effort and communication.

  • My first year at Mapúa

    So far, my first year studying Computer Engineering at Mapúa has been intense, but it's also made me sure of my choice. Even with the heavy workload and challenging problem sets, working through them has forced me to change my study habits. I've learned that just memorizing things doesn't cut it anymore. You actually have to understand how the concepts work and be okay with figuring things out as you go.

  • Looking ahead

    I'm really interested in getting deeper into areas like automation, smart systems, and AI. The idea of building tech that actually solves practical problems is what keeps me motivated.

  • How I've grown

    Even with the late nights and tricky assignments, I can see how much I've improved. I'm getting better at handling difficult problems, breaking down complex tasks, and working with others. It's a challenging course, but it's definitely helping me build the right skills for the future.
